# PEERSPECT

**Your guide to how places actually feel.**

- **Role:** Product designer, Ginko Emdon
- **Team:** Ginko, with Muse (Wynn, AI collaborator)

## 1. The problem: digging through reviews to find how a place feels

**Instead of digging through reviews to find experiential insights, PEERSPECT helps you target your needs through filters to discover what places align.**

- Reviews bury the experience under hundreds of opinions
- You have to guess how a place will feel for you, in your body, on your day
- Too loud, too bright, or hard to get into, and reading reviews will not tell you that
- I started with the people reviews fail most, disabled and neurodiverse people first
- Needs change day by day. A place that worked on a quiet morning can fail on a loud afternoon

## 2. The solution: find a place by how it feels

**PEERSPECT adds a peer layer to business listings, so you can tell if a place matches what you need today, before you go.**

- Start with a type of day, or pick what matters to you
- See only the places that fit, nearest first, on a map or in a list
- While you are there, the app asks one simple question at a time, anonymous
- If a peer reported something earlier, it asks if it is still true
- Businesses see a calm dashboard of patterns and gentle suggestions
- Never raw data, never a name

[Visual: three screens, Discover, I'm here, Business, side by side.]

## 3. My approach: AI-first prototyping, with me deciding

**I prototype in a working app, not in static mockups, so every decision is tested on a real interaction the same day.**

The loop:

- I think, then I prompt
- Muse builds it working
- I use it on my phone, I judge it, and I change it the same day

How I prompt:

- Short and specific, one decision at a time
- Reasoning and constraint in the prompt, so the AI builds what I meant
- Accessibility is a constraint from the start, not a check at the end

What the working app taught me:

- A three pick limit worked against agency, so I cut it
- Coloured dots looked wrong on real cards, so I removed them
- A bar stayed only when it showed a real count against a stated total

The AI did not decide anything for me. It built what I asked for, and I corrected it when it got my thinking wrong. The prototype moves fast because the AI builds fast, and it stays mine because I judge every version.

## 4. How my thinking got there

**Four beats, each one following from the last.**

- Reviews hide the experience, so I made it visible through filters
  - Sound, light, crowding, access, in plain words
  - Target what you need, see what aligns
- Needs change day by day, so the app never asks who you are
  - It asks what matters today
  - Change the answer tomorrow
- Trust needs identity removed
  - Reports are anonymous, always
  - Checked by peers, a report earns trust only when another peer confirms it
- AI should inform, not decide
  - Every suggestion shows the data behind it
  - Agency and autonomy go hand in hand with customizability

An honest limit: this was AI-first design, so I did not consult lived experts or run user research for this prototype. The decisions came from my own reasoning and from testing the working app myself. Testing with real users is the next step, not something I can claim I have already done.
