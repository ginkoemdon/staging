PEERSPECT: Content Standards

How we write in PEERSPECT, so every screen feels warm, clear, and easy to use. These standards apply to every screen, in light and dark.

Brand: warm, human, steady guide. Informed autonomy. Easy, simple, intuitive, clear. Reduce overwhelm and decision fatigue. Build trust and confidence. Colours: warm white #fdfbf7, teal #14665a, pine #2f5d3e, honey #f2c14e, amber #8a4a08, ink #1a2b26. Fonts: Atkinson Hyperlegible headings, Inter body, 16px, line height 1.6. Spacing: iOS, 16pt side margins, 20pt sections, 44pt targets.

1. Tone and voice

Tone and voice: warm, human, steady guide

Write like a kind guide walking beside the reader. Warm and human, never clinical, never hype. Soft, gentle language. No fluff, no pressure, no promises we cannot keep.

Do

Take your time.

Pick what matters to you today. You can change it any time.

Do not

Unlock your perfect day now!

Our revolutionary AI will optimise your lifestyle in seconds.

2. Plain language

Plain language: short, clear, one idea at a time

Use short sentences. One idea per line. Plain words first. Explain a specialist term the first time it appears, in the same sentence if you can.

Do

This place has step-free entry.

That means no steps at the door. A ramp or flat entry is there instead.

Do not

This venue demonstrates exemplary universal design compliance across multiple accessibility vectors, facilitating unimpeded ingress for diverse mobility profiles.

3. Onboarding wording

Onboarding: What matters to you today

Ask what matters today, not how the person feels. Let them pick as many as they like. Agency first. They can add, remove, or change choices any time, and save them as a profile.

Do

What matters to you today?

Pick what matters, as many as you like. You decide.

Step-free entrySoft lightQuietLow scent

Do not

How do you feel today?

Pick up to 3 only. You cannot change this later.

Step-free entry

4. Place descriptions

Place descriptions: by how a place feels

Describe a place by how it feels, using these factors:

  • Access
  • Sensory experience
  • Environment
  • Context

Not by stars, and not by long reviews. Name what is true, and when it is true, because places change through the day.

Do

Cafe Lumiere Douce

Calm in the morning. Soft light. Quiet corners. Step-free entry.

This fits today

0.8 km, 10 min walk, Metro Jarry

Do not

Cafe Lumiere Douce: 4.8 stars

Amazing!!! Best cafe ever, everyone loves it, you HAVE to go, the vibes are unmatched and the owner is so sweet, I went there last Tuesday and...

5. Check-in questions

Check-in questions: one at a time, easy to answer

Ask one simple question at a time. Simple multiple choice, with answers that fit the question asked. Low cognitive load. Anonymous. Easy to skip. No scoring, no bombardment.

Do

Question 1 of 3, anonymous

How is the sound right now?

Answers fit the question. "How is the light right now?" Bright / Just right / Too dim. "How crowded is it?" Quiet / A few people / Busy. "Is the entry step-free?" Yes / No, there is a step / Not sure.

Do not

Rate this venue

On a scale of 1 to 10, how would you rate the acoustic environment, lighting quality, spatial layout, staff responsiveness, and overall inclusivity, with detailed comments for each?

6. Trust signals

Trust signals: peer confirmed, never personal

Show that peers agree, without identifying anyone. Use confirmed by X peers, and badges like Reliable observer. Never name an individual reporter. Make it easy to report misuse.

Do

Confirmed by 12 peers

Reports from Reliable observers hold more weight.

Report a problem

Do not

Alex M. reported this at 2:14 pm. Alex has 98 trust points and lives nearby.

Click here to see who reported what.

7. Business suggestions

Business suggestions: visual, pickable, kind

Show business owners what peers experience, visually and intuitively. No raw data as the default. Offer pre-written options they can pick, so they can act without dissecting data.

Do

Calm in the morning

Evenings are louder

Do not

Raw data export: n=412, mean=6.42, SD=1.87, p=0.03, decibel variance by hour, full CSV attached. Analyse it yourself.

8. Empty states, errors, and loading

Empty states, errors, and loading: kind, clear next step

When nothing is found, or something goes wrong, say so kindly. Give one clear next step. No dead ends. No blame. Loading is brief, honest, and skippable.

Do

No places match yet

Try removing one filter, or widen the distance a little.

Do not

Error 404: No results

You chose the wrong filters. Try again.

9. Labels and buttons

Labels and buttons: clear action, one next step

Every button says what it does, in plain words. One clear next step per screen. Buttons are at least 44pt tall, easy to tap, and easy to see.

Do

One clear next step, in plain words.

Do not

Three small links, none saying what happens next.

10. Sample data labelling

Sample data: label it clearly, never present it as real

Sample places, sample quotes, and sample data are labelled as sample, every time. No invented quotes, names, or results presented as real. No account needed for the demo.

Do

Demo: sample places in Montreal only

These places are examples, to show how PEERSPECT works. No account needed.

Do not

"I love this place, it changed my life!" says a regular, who asked to stay anonymous, in a real review from last week.

Checklist

Checklist: before new content ships

Use this list for every new screen, message, or place description.

  • Is the tone warm, human, and steady, not clinical or hype?
  • Is it plain language, short sentences, one idea at a time?
  • Is every line of supporting text absolutely necessary and helpful? It should add important context that helps the user understand the next step, the card, or whatever it is on. If it does not, and it has unnecessary information, cut it. It should not take up space.
  • Does it ask what matters today, and let the person choose freely?
  • Does it describe how a place feels, not just stars or long reviews?
  • Is a check-in one question at a time, with answers that fit the question asked?
  • Are trust signals peer confirmed, with no one identified?
  • Are business suggestions visual and pickable, not raw data first?
  • Do empty states and errors offer one kind, clear next step?
  • Do buttons say what they do, with one clear next step per screen?
  • Is sample data clearly labelled as sample?
  • Does it pass WCAG 2.2 AA in light and dark?
  • Is there generous iOS spacing, 16pt margins, 44pt targets?
  • Are there no em dashes in headings?

Demo note: all places, people, and quotes on this page are samples, to show the standards in use. No account needed.
