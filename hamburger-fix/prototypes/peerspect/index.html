PEERSPECT

Your guide to how places actually feel.

At a glance

Filter establishments by how they feel, not by stars, before you go. Designed by Ginko, with Muse, in November 2026, as a mobile-first prototype.

  • RoleProduct designer, Ginko Emdon
  • TeamGinko, with Muse (Wynn, AI collaborator)
  • WhoDisabled and neurodiverse people first, and the business owners who serve them. Useful for everyone.
  • TypeMobile-first web app prototype
  • StatusIn progress, November 2026
  • ScopeConcept prototype. Twelve real Montreal cafés, with sample peer data. No live listing data, no user testing yet.
  • ToolsHTML, CSS, JavaScript, sample data. Google Places API, not yet connected.

Context

A peer layer on business listings, so you can tell how an establishment feels before you go.

What

Experiential needs, matched to establishment and business listings, not reviews.

Who

Disabled and neurodiverse people first, and business owners who want to understand them. Useful for everyone.

When

Concept and prototype, November 2026.

How

Anonymous peer check-ins, one tap at a time.

Problem

Reviews give stars, not how an establishment feels, so people cannot tell if it fits today.

What stars show

★★★★★

One number. No light, sound, crowding, or access.

What people need

SoundQuiet
LightBright
CrowdingCalm
AccessStep-free

A star is a judgement, not a description. That gap costs disabled and neurodiverse people a wasted trip, and costs businesses visitors they never hear from.

Solution

Three moments, not five features. Each one is a real situation, and what PEERSPECT does in it.

Underneath all three: 92 needs in six groups, Access, Sensory, Environment, Context, Inclusion, and Menu. Returning users can start from a saved profile, or last time's picks, in one tap.

Process

I started from a lived question, then let the design prove itself in the app, one decision at a time.

  1. 1

    Ask the question

    I could not tell from reviews if a place would work for me that day.

  2. 2

    Name the model

    One star became four groups: Access, Sensory, Environment, Context.

  3. 3

    See it, don't list it

    One question at a time, options I could see, not just read. Onboarding starts with a day, not a blank list of 92 needs.

  4. 4

    Fix the words

    Yes or Not really did not answer how loud it was. Sound became level plus type, and type became multi-select, because sounds happen at once.

  5. 5

    Build the system

    Palette, type, spacing, and content rules together. Accessibility as a rule.

  6. 6

    Break it, fix it

    The first version felt like a page on my site. I rebuilt the frame so the app feels like its own world.

Key decisions

Four choices that kept the design simple, customisable, anonymous, and visual.

Tap a card to open it.

Four groups, not one long list

A long list creates overload. Four groups are easier to scan.

Trade-off: some needs fit two groups. I placed each once, to keep scanning fast.

No pick limit

A three-pick limit worked against agency. Accessibility means customisability.

Trade-off: more picks can dilute a match, so a place needs at least half of them, minimum two.

Anonymous prompts, not named reviews

Low effort and safer, with peer confirmation for trust. “Still” questions now show the earlier report first, so the prompt makes sense.

Trade-off: no follow-up with the reporter, so peer confirmation has to carry the trust instead.

Visual dashboard, not raw data

Owners see patterns and suggestions, never who said what.

Trade-off: owners get less detail, but a suggestion they can act on.

Branding and UI kit

A warm, human, steady guide, in its own colours, not my site colours.

Testing, lessons, and next steps

Not yet measured, and that is honest. Here is what to test next, and what I learned building it.

No user testing, no metrics, none claimed here. Next: is the quick start clear, do the presets feel right, is multi-select sound clear, do the “still” questions make sense, and is the dashboard readable.

What I learned: a tidy limit can work against agency, and agency goes hand in hand with customisability.

  • Test onboarding, groups, check-ins, and the business dashboard.
  • Build the live listing connection, with real peer data.
