Consent-Aware Research Assistant

Summaries you can trace back to what someone actually said — and nothing is used unless they agreed to share it.

How to use this product

  1. Check the consent switches — each of the 5 sample notes is marked Shareable or Private. Flip one to Private and its words are hidden here.
  2. Press “Synthesise insights” — only Shareable notes are used, and every insight links back to the exact note it came from.
  3. Read what it does not know — the gaps are listed below, and Private notes are named as excluded, never quoted or counted.

What happens: the count line updates to show how many notes were included and which were excluded, and the insights change to match. Sample data only. No backend, no external AI calls. Nothing you type or toggle is sent anywhere.

Prototype — sample data. These 5 interview notes are invented examples, not real participants. No data leaves your browser, and no external AI is called.

Step 3 — What it does not know

What was NOT found

This is what the sample does not support. A good research assistant says this out loud, instead of filling the gap with a guess.

  • No evidence that people want longer, more detailed explanations. Nobody in these 5 notes asked for more detail — they asked for straighter, shorter wording.
  • No evidence about price, or about desktop vs. mobile. Those topics were not in the notes, so this synthesis says nothing about them.
  • No claim about how common these views are. This is 5 sample notes, not a representative study. “2 of 5 notes” is a count of this sample, not a statistic about all users.
  • Marlowe’s view cannot be quoted or counted while their consent is off — even though it is the note most directly about trust. That gap is part of the finding, not a bug to work around.

Prompt design

The instruction behind it

What the assistant is told before it synthesises — shown here so you can judge it, not hidden away.

System instruction (sample):

“Link every insight to a source. Do not invent quotes. Say what you don't know.”

Plus, enforced in this demo: only use notes marked Shareable. If a source is Private, exclude it entirely and say that you did.

Try it: turn Marlowe back to Shareable, re-run the synthesis, and watch Insight 3 gain a second source — then turn Sol Private, and watch Insight 3 disappear.
